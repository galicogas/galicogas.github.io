Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. Water Heater Replacement

Q: When should I replace my water heater?

Most traditional tanks last 8–12 years. You should consider a replacement if your unit is over 10 years old, shows visible rust or leaks at the base, or if you notice rumbling noises caused by sediment buildup.

Q: What is the difference between a tank and tankless water heater?

Standard tanks are more affordable upfront but have higher monthly energy costs. Tankless models offer endless hot water and 24–34% better energy efficiency, though they require a higher initial investment for unit and installation.

Q: Is it better to repair or replace my unit?

We recommend the 50% rule: if a repair costs more than half the price of a new unit, replacement is the better investment. Note that if the tank itself is leaking, it cannot be repaired and must be replaced.

Q: How long does a professional installation take?

A standard tank-for-tank swap usually takes 2–4 hours. Converting from a tank to a tankless system can take 6+ hours as it often requires upgrades to your gas lines, venting, or electrical panel.

Q: Can a new water heater save me money on utilities?

Yes. Modern high-efficiency units, especially Heat Pump (Hybrid) and Tankless models, can save homeowners between $100 and $200 annually. Many residents in Ontario also qualify for energy efficiency rebates.
